Help & FAQ

Most problems can be fixed in a minute. Tap a question to see the answer.

The compass

The arrow does not turn when I turn
  • iPhone (iOS 13 or later): tap the green Enable compass button, then Allow.
  • You tapped “Cancel” or “Don’t Allow”: Safari will not ask again straight away. Close Safari completely (swipe it away from the app switcher), open the page again and tap Enable compass.
  • iPhone with iOS 12: go to Settings › Safari › Motion & Orientation Access, turn it on, then reload the page.
  • Android: use Chrome and make sure the page address starts with https://.
  • Laptop or desktop computer: computers have no compass sensor, so the arrow cannot turn. The arrow then shows the Qibla measured from North (the red N). Open the page on your phone instead.
The arrow seems wrong or jumps around

Phone compasses sense the Earth’s magnetic field, so nearby metal and magnets can throw them off.

  • Take off magnetic cases, MagSafe wallets and magnetic car mounts.
  • Move away from laptops, speakers, fridges, metal desks and cars (step out of the car).
  • Calibrate: move your phone slowly in a figure-of-8 a few times.
  • Hold the phone flat, screen facing up, at waist height.
  • Basements, lifts and underground trains are full of steel and wiring. Check the direction outside or near a window, then note a landmark.
  • If in doubt, check via other Qibla apps to verify.
Another Qibla app shows a slightly different direction

A difference of a few degrees is normal. It comes from the phone’s compass, not the calculation: this app uses the standard great-circle direction to the Kaaba, the same method most Qibla apps use. Try the tips in “The arrow seems wrong” above and compare again in the same spot. The app says “Facing the Qibla” when you are within 5°.

Your location

It says location is blocked or denied
  • iPhone: Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services must be on, then Safari Websites › While Using the App. Go back to the page and tap Update my location.
  • Android (Chrome): tap the icon to the left of the web address › Permissions › Location › Allow.
  • Laptop: click the icon to the left of the web address › Location › Allow, then reload. On Windows also check Settings › Privacy & security › Location.
  • Don’t want to share your location? That’s fine. Type your city in the box that appears instead. For the Qibla, your city is accurate enough.
It shows the wrong city

When exact location is not allowed, the app guesses your city from your internet connection. With a VPN, mobile data or some Wi-Fi networks that guess can be another city or even another country. Turn off the VPN and tap Update my location, allow location access, or type your city in the box.

“Update my location” does not change anything

The button shows what happened for a few seconds (for example “Location updated” or “Approximate location”). If location is blocked, the phone refuses straight away. Allow location as explained above. If you have not moved far, the direction stays the same. That is expected.

I am travelling (car, train, plane)

The app finds your location when it opens. On a long journey, tap Update my location now and then. Inside vehicles the metal body upsets the compass, so check the direction when you stop.

Using the app

The map does not show

The map is optional; you never need it to find the Qibla. It uses free OpenStreetMap maps, and because this app is free, the map is shared by a limited number of people each day. When the daily limit is reached, the map returns the next day. The compass and city search keep working.

Can I install it like an app?

Yes. iPhone: in Safari tap Share › Add to Home Screen. Android: in Chrome tap ⋮ › Add to Home screen. After your first visit the compass also works without internet.

Privacy: what happens to my data?
  • Your exact location stays on your device. It is only used to work out the direction and is remembered on your phone for next time.
  • No sign-up, no ads, no cookies, no personal data collected.
  • The app counts anonymous daily totals (how many devices opened the app and the map today) with the free Abacus counter. No location or personal details are sent.
  • If you type a city, that text is sent to OpenStreetMap Nominatim to find it. If location is blocked, GeoJS estimates your city from your internet connection. Map pictures come from OpenStreetMap.
  • The app is open source: anyone can check the code.
